Shake-Flask kLa Estimator

Estimate the volumetric oxygen transfer coefficient (kLa) for orbitally shaken Erlenmeyer flasks from the validated Seletzky/Maier correlation, and see instantly whether your operating point falls inside its published domain.

No universal correlation exists for baffled flasks. The equation is validated for standard non-baffled glass Erlenmeyer flasks only. For baffled geometries, this tool applies a correction factor you supply from your own calibration (sulfite oxidation, dynamic gassing-out, RAMOS/OTR) — it does not predict a baffled value from first principles. Full sourcing is at the bottom of the page.

Methodology & validity checks

Base equation (Seletzky/Maier)

kLa [s⁻¹] = 6.67×10⁻⁶ · n1.16 · VL−0.83 · d₀0.38 · d1.92, where n = rpm, VL = working volume (mL), d₀ = orbit diameter (cm), d = flask max. inner diameter (cm). Result ×3600 gives h⁻¹. Computed server-side in kla.py — see the GitHub repo.

Validated domain

Standard glass Erlenmeyer flasks, 50–500 rpm, 4–20% fill, orbit diameter 1.25–10 cm, nominal flask size 50–1000 mL. Reported accuracy ≈ ±30% within this domain.

Baffled flasks

No universal correlation exists for arbitrary baffle geometries. This tool multiplies the base non-baffled estimate by a correction factor you supply from your own measurement — it is a placeholder for a real calibration, not a prediction.

Temperature

Recorded as a process input for your records only; it does not enter the base kLa equation used here.

Sources

This estimator reproduces the correlation, unit convention, and validity ranges documented in the original Shake_Flask_kLa_Calculator.xlsx assumptions sheet:

  1. Seletzky, J. — dissertation, RWTH Aachen. Source of the equation, units, validated operating range and ~±30% accuracy statement. publications.rwth-aachen.de
  2. Brauneck et al., 2025, Engineering in Life Sciences — reproduces the shake-flask kLa correlation and discusses operating parameters. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
  3. Klöckner & Büchs, Comprehensive Biotechnology — baffled flasks can increase oxygen transfer, but no general mathematical correlation exists as a function of baffle geometry. sciencedirect.com
  4. Maier & Büchs, 2001 — foundational characterization of gas–liquid mass transfer in orbital shake flasks. doi.org/10.1016/S1369-703X(00)00107-8

Generic flask diameters (50/100/250/500/1000 mL → 5/7/8/10/12 cm) are illustrative values from the source workbook. Replace with your flask manufacturer's stated maximum inner diameter for a more accurate estimate. All results are engineering estimates, not direct measurements — verify with your own oxygen-transfer calibration whenever precision matters.
